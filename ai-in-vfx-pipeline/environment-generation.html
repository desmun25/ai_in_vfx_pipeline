AI vs Manual HDRI Generation

Introduction

The aim of this test will be to compare an HDRI skydome created by me using a program called PTGui, with an AI generated one created using Hyper 3D's HDRI generator, using a reference image of the real world location I was attempting to match.

Plan

The plan for this test was that I would re-use an HDRI I created for one of my A level productions of the desert near my home in Qatar and compare those results two those of the AI generated model. There were several reasons for this. The first is that one of the main reasons that this interested me is that HDRI generation is a laborious, difficult process.

To create an HDRI you most place a camera on a tripod and use bracketed exposures to photograph 3 photos for each angle you photograph, one at a normal exposure, one under exposed, and one over exposed. You must repeat this with the camera pointed in every direction to capture the environment in 360*. These sets of three images are then each combined into a single HDR (High Dynamic Range) image, and these HDR images are then stitched together into a 360* panorama using a program of your choice, in my case PTGui, largely because it is free to use.

That process is as long and boring as it sounds and is precisely why I hoped that an AI solution might be more efficient. The second reason for me wanting to conduct the test using my previously created HDRI is that it allows me to create a 1 for 1 comparison in a production environment. As with the corridor key test, being able to directly compare results is valuable, especially when there are obvious technical flaws in my work due to the limitations of being a singe creator with a limited budget. My HDRI was not truly HDR, it did not have the dynamic range necessary to accurately replicate the light in the environment, meaning that I had to supplement the HDRI with a sun lamp in order to produce the shots.

Perhaps an AI generated HDRI based on a reference image of the environment it must match, will be able to speed up this process and overcome some of the pitfalls of my bodged together HDRI?

Expectations / Hypothesis

I anticipate that the AI generated HDRI will less accurately depict the environment that the scene is supposed to match, as the photographic reference it will be basing it's generation on is only used as reference, rather than directly as images to stitch together into the final result. However, I expect that it's stitching will be almost flawless, and that the HDRI's it produces will be truly HDR, and will be able to light the scene without the need for any additional CG lights, unlike the HDRI I was able to create myself using traditional methods.

Results

Overall, it is clear that the results of this test were a fail for the AI. There are several problems, the biggest of which is that the end results were not truly HDR (High Dynamic Range) meaning that to look anywhere near good the need to be supplemented with additional lights inside of blender, failing to overcome one of the key problems with my traditionally created HDRI.

Additionally, even when supported with additional lighting, the results that it produces are clearly of worse quality than the original. They do not look nearly as accurate because the lighting in the AI generated world is not physically based, meaning that you cannot place a light in the scene approximating in the position of the sun and expect it to light your objects realistically, as the shadows cast by objects in the background are not motivated by a sun, and do not even maintain a consistent direction themselves.

While laborious, it is clear that for now, the process of manually creating an HDRI is the best way to go for this sort of thing in order to produce realistic, high quality results.
