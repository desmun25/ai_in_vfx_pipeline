Corridor Key vs DaVinci Resolve's 3D Keyer

Introduction

The aim of this test will be to compare the results of Corridor Key, a machine learning based green screening tool, with the results of Davinci Resolve's 3D keyer, one of the most effective and easily accessible green screen solutions available to VFX artists.

Plan

To conduct this test, I will experiment on a series of Green Screen shots using original footage and background plates from two of my short films, "Everyone's Afraid of the Dark" (2024) and "Black Listed" (2025).

I will recomposite each shot exactly as I did for the original versions, with the only difference being that the green screen keying will be completed using Corridor Key (Specifically the OFX v0.7.3 plugin enabling corridor key to run within Davinci Resolve, details available here: https://github.com/alexandremendoncaalvaro/CorridorKey-Runtime/releases/tag/v0.7.3 ).

These shots will have a variety of issues, including motion blur, unevenly lit backgrounds and soft focus, characteristics that can be highly problematic with traditional keying methodologies, in order to push corridor key and find out how it handles aspects that make shots difficult or impossible ordinarily.

Presentation of findings

Unfortunately, the results of these tests will be difficult, if not impossible, to present in a quantitative manner. As such, I feel it is best to instead opt for a more qualitative analysis. To do this while still remaining as objective as possible, I will look at each shot from 3 different points of view: Visual fidelity (A personal judgement of the final shot and which looks more realistic), Technical fidelity (Comparing the Alpha Maps to better analyse how elements such as motion blur and soft focus are handled), and Efficacy of Process (how simple the process of generating the result is). For each of these categories, on each shot, a "point" (for lack of a better term) will be awarded. These "points" can then be accounted for and allow for a somewhat quantitative finding to be presented, demonstrating alongside the qualitative analysis which the better methodology is, and by what margin.

Expectations / Hypothesis

I anticipate from the research I have done, that Corridor Key will offer a higher quality end result, handily winning out in visual and technical fidelity particularly when handling the aforementioned issues of motion blur, poor lighting and soft focus. Where I expect more traditional keying may still hold it's own is in efficacy of process.

Corridor key requires an "Alpha Hint" a traditionally keyed version of the shot which can be imperfect, but which must be done before the AI version can produce the final key. Because of this, corridor key in effect adds an additional step to the keying pipeline. While the alpha hint not needing to be perfect means that less time needs to be spent on the keyed version, I am not sure whether the time savings from doing less "fine-toothed-comb" clean up work will be enough to make corridor key more efficient, particularly when working with shots that are already well optimised for Green Screen Keying.

In conclusion, I am expecting Corridor Key to yield higher quality results, while only making negligible (if any) improvements to the efficiency and efficacy of the keying process.

Conclusion

Overall, it is clear that Corridor Key produces higher quality results than traditional keying. It handles details such as motion blur much better, as well as fine details such as hair (where it sometimes reduces the definition of the detail while reducing temporal flickering, giving a more pleasing end result.

However, unlike the fast majority of AI tools, it does not improve the speed of the workflow (efficacy of process in scoring). Unlike what I had predicted, having to produce the alpha hint using traditional keying methodologies does not actually slow you down too much, as you can be far more “rough and ready” than if you were to produce a final shot using traditional keying; the real bottleneck is in the rendering of the shots.

Where traditional keying can be played back almost in real time, and rasterised in seconds, real time playback with corridor key is utterly impossible, and rendering out a shot of a approximately 10 seconds could take upwards of ten minutes (using an intel core i5 and an Nvidia RTX GPU). This slows down the rate of iteration and means that if you were to integrate this tool into a production pipeline, you would be better off iterating rough versions using traditional keying to take advantage of faster playback, and then using Corridor Key to produce a final high fidelity version of the shot.
